    Research question
    Can changing which scientific documents a compact model sees improve performance on uncommon research categories under a fixed training budget?
    Observed trade-off
    Across five seeds, tail-aware validation tail perplexity averaged 47.33 compared with 51.82 for random selection. Random had lower overall loss. Lower perplexity is better, but these findings apply to this corpus, model, and budget.
    

    The data experiment
    Five ways to choose data.
    Every strategy selects approximately 30M unique training tokens from the same corpus. The selection rules change which documents make the cut.
    Training mix
    Group shares are calculated from selected training tokens. Tail categories contain fewer than 1,000 training documents.
    What is the long tail?
    Scientific categories vary greatly in size. Categories with fewer than 1,000 training documents form the tail in this study. A naturally sampled subset devotes most of its tokens to larger categories, leaving fewer examples from uncommon fields.
    How the allocation changes
    The sampler assigns topic budgets using available token counts raised to a power. An exponent below one flattens the distribution, increasing the relative share of smaller categories. Budgets are capped by available data and documents are selected under the same token limit.
    What the comparison isolates
    Random provides a natural-distribution baseline. Quality tests filtering. Diversity balances text themes. Tail-aware combines filtering with category-based allocation. Tail-only applies category-based allocation without the quality filter. Five training seeds share one selected subset per strategy, so they measure training variation rather than subset-sampling variation.
    

    Inside the system
    From papers to predictions.
    A reproducible pipeline turns arXiv titles and abstracts into a compact causal language model.
    01 / CORPUS
    Collect & clean
    Filter the pinned arXiv snapshot; normalize, deduplicate and split by document ID.
    02 / SELECTION
    Score & sample
    Quality heuristics, category frequency and text themes define five comparable subsets.
    03 / TOKENIZE
    Byte-level BPE
    16,384-token shared vocabulary. Each title and abstract ends with one EOT token.
    04 / EMBEDDINGS
    Token + position
    384-dimensional token embeddings plus learned position embeddings; context length 512.
    05 / TRANSFORMER x 6
    Attend & transform
    Pre-layer normalization, 6-head causal self-attention, residual connections and GELU MLP.
    06 / OUTPUT
    Next-token prediction
    Final layer normalization and tied output embeddings produce vocabulary logits.
    The model is pretrained on scientific titles and abstracts; it is not an instruction-tuned assistant.
    Training objective
    The decoder predicts each next token from preceding tokens using causal attention. Cross-entropy loss updates the shared parameters. The model sees 512-token contexts and is trained for approximately 60 million tokens per run.
    What stays fixed
    The five strategies share the tokenizer, architecture, optimizer settings, validation data, and token budget. Changes in validation performance can be studied against the training subset while acknowledging other sources of uncertainty.
    From checkpoint to application
    The research checkpoints measure next-token performance. Separate drafting and title fine-tuning pilots adapt a random-strategy checkpoint to structured input. The drafting workspace can preserve the supplied sentences or display model wording for review. These pilots do not establish that tail-aware pretraining improves drafting.
